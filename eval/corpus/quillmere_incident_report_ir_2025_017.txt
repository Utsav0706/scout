Quillmere Robotics Ltd - Incident Report IR-2025-017
Title: Warehouse robot fleet stoppage at the Daventry fulfilment site
Date of incident: 14 July 2025. Severity: SEV-1. Status: closed.
Incident commander: Hana Lindqvist. Report author: Tomas Reyes.
FICTIONAL DOCUMENT created for the Scout evaluation set. Quillmere Robotics is not a real company.

1. Summary
At 06:18 BST all 64 AX-7 picking robots at the Daventry site stopped at once and stayed stopped. Picking resumed in stages and full service returned at 10:00 BST. The total outage lasted 3 hours 42 minutes.

2. Impact
- 64 AX-7 robots stopped (the whole Daventry fleet).
- About 11,200 customer orders were delayed by at least half a day.
- Two customers had their service level agreements breached: Brindle Foods and Orrin Pharma.
- Quillmere paid service credits totalling GBP 18,500 to affected customers.
- No one was injured and no stock was damaged.

3. Timeline (all times BST)
05:55 Fleet controller "Conductor" automatically updated from version 5.2.4 to version 5.3.0.
06:18 All robots triggered an emergency stop within 4 seconds of each other.
06:31 Warehouse floor staff reported the stoppage to the operations desk. Monitoring raised no alert. The stoppage was therefore detected 13 minutes after it began.
06:45 Incident declared SEV-1; Hana Lindqvist took command.
08:10 Engineers linked the stoppage to the Conductor update.
09:40 Conductor rolled back to version 5.2.4.
09:40-10:00 Robots restarted in batches of 8 so each batch could pass safety checks.
10:00 Full service restored.

4. Root cause
Conductor version 5.3.0 changed the interval between heartbeat messages sent to each robot from 2 seconds to 20 seconds, as part of an effort to reduce network traffic.
Every AX-7 runs a safety watchdog that triggers an emergency stop if it receives no heartbeat for 5 seconds. With heartbeats now 20 seconds apart, every robot's watchdog fired and the whole fleet stopped.
The change passed all existing tests because no test compared the controller's heartbeat interval with the robots' watchdog timeout.

5. Why detection was slow
Our monitoring checked that the Conductor service was running, which it was. It did not alert on robots entering emergency stop.

6. Actions
A1. Add a contract test that fails if the heartbeat interval is not well below the robot watchdog timeout. Owner: Tomas Reyes. Due: 31 August 2025. Status: done.
A2. Roll out Conductor updates to 10 percent of a site's fleet first, and wait 30 minutes before the rest. Owner: Hana Lindqvist. Due: 15 September 2025. Status: done.
A3. Page the on-call engineer when more than 5 robots enter emergency stop within 1 minute. Owner: Site Reliability team. Due: 31 July 2025. Status: done.
A4. Stop automatic controller updates during picking hours (05:00-22:00). Owner: Operations. Status: done.

7. Lessons
Two safe settings can be unsafe together. Safety-related timing values must be tested as a pair.
