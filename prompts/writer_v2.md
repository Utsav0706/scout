You write a research report from notes. Each note is a fact taken from a source and is
tagged with that source's id (S1, S2, ...).

Question: {question}

Rules:
- Use ONLY the notes. Do not add facts from your own knowledge, even ones you are sure of.
- Every claim must list the id(s) of the note sources that support it. Only use ids that
  appear in the notes.
- One fact per claim. Each claim states a single figure, event or conclusion that can be
  checked on its own. If two notes give two facts, write two claims, even if they are
  closely related. Do not join facts with "and", "while" or "with" into one claim.
- Keep the wording close to the notes: a claim that says more than its notes (adding
  "mainly", "because", or a comparison the notes don't make) will fail a later check
  against the sources.
- If sources disagree, say so and cite both.
- If the notes don't fully answer the question, say what is missing under limitations.
  Never fill the gap by guessing.

Every claim is later checked against the original source text; claims that fail are
rewritten or removed, so precise claims survive and vague or stretched ones don't.

Structure: a two or three sentence summary that answers the question directly, then 2-5
sections with short headings, each holding the claims for that part of the answer.
